# LLM-era quest generation, story direction and event injection (2023–2026): research and engineering patterns

Scope note: academic and open research only. Commercial products appear only where they publish architecture. Status tags: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed venue (journal, main conference, or refereed workshop); **[PP]** = arXiv preprint, no venue found; **[BLOG]** = developer or critic blog or docs; **[DEMO]** = demo or proof of concept with no systematic evaluation. Wherever a number appears, it comes from the cited source. Points that are my own inference sit under "Inferences".

---

## 1. Which papers and systems exist for LLM quest generation, story direction and emergent events?

### Takeaway
The field moved from "fine-tune GPT-2 to write quest text" (2022–23, about 20% acceptable) to director architectures (2024–26). In these, an LLM fills or instantiates **author-defined structure**: abstract acts, storylets or triggers, beats, event graphs, knowledge trees. A separate state keeper, symbolic or LLM, holds the facts. Systems that are pure LLMs with no structure are now mostly used as baselines that lose.

### Cited Findings

**Quest generators (content generation)**
- Värtinen, Hämäläinen, Guckelsberger, "Generating Role-Playing Game Quests With GPT Language Models", IEEE Transactions on Games 16(1):127–139, 2024 (DOI 10.1109/TG.2022.3228480) **[PR]**. They fine-tuned GPT-2 ("Quest-GPT-2") on 978 quests from 6 RPGs (the dataset is published) and ran GPT-3 case studies. In an online study with 349 RPG players, about **1 in 5 generated quest descriptions would be acceptable to a human critic**, with large variance between quests. — [Aalto research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/generating-role-playing-game-quests-with-gpt-language-models/)
- Ashby, Webb, Knapp, Searle, Fulda, "Personalized Quest and Dialogue Generation in Role-Playing Games: A Knowledge Graph- and Language Model-based Approach", CHI 2023 (DOI 10.1145/3544548.3581441) **[PR]**. A **hand-crafted knowledge base grounds quest content in game context**, and the LM only writes the accompanying dialogue. In human evaluation the quests "approach" hand-crafted ones on fluency, coherence, novelty and creativity. The authors also propose an automated quest–dialogue relevance metric. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3544548.3581441?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,citationCount)
- Ammanabrolu et al., "Toward Automated Quest Generation in Text-Adventure Games", CCNLG@INLG 2019 **[PR, pre-LLM era]**. Quests are defined as "a series of actions required to progress towards a goal". The paper compares Markov chains with a neural generator on cooking quests trained on recipe data, using human ratings of creativity and coherence. This is the pre-LLM baseline lineage, from the same group as the later LIGHT work. — [arXiv 1909.06283](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06283v2)
- Borawski et al., "From World-Gen to Quest-Line: A Dependency-Driven Prompt Pipeline for Coherent RPG Generation", Apr 2026 **[PP]**. Staged pipeline: world → NPCs → player character → campaign quest plan → quest expansion. Each stage is conditioned on validated JSON from earlier stages. The raw output is kept alongside the validated JSON so that schema violations and narrative drift can be debugged. In qualitative human analysis the pipeline produced "logically sound and structurally valid" content with no degradation as complexity grew. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2604.25482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25482)
- Story2Game (Georgia Tech, Riedl group), May 2025 **[PP]**. GPT-4o-mini expands 2–6 seed events into a 5–18 event story, then annotates every action with **preconditions** (fundamental: location and inventory; additional: attributes such as `chest.unopened == False`; preceding events) and **effects** (move, attribute change, create object, remove object). These annotations drive generation of action code over a world graph. Results: 92.8–97.2% of actions compile, but only **62.5–87.5% of whole stories compile**. For novel player actions generated on the fly, about 80% compile and **about 60% are semantically correct**. — [arXiv 2505.03547](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03547v1)
- Retrieval-augmented local Llama 3.2 for quest generation and NPC personality consistency (JAIC, Politeknik Negeri Batam, 2025) **[PR, minor regional journal]**. Fully local RAG over a JSON vector DB, comparing MiniLM, FastText and BPE retrieval. I could not fetch the full text (HTTP 500), so no numbers. — [JAIC article](https://jurnal.polibatam.ac.id/index.php/JAIC/article/view/13560)

**Story directors and drama managers**
- StoryVerse (Wang, Zhou, Ledo; Autodesk Research), FDG 2024 **[PR, DEMO-level eval]**. Writers author **abstract acts** with three parts: a narrative goal (e.g. "a character gets into a life-threatening accident"), **prerequisites** (logical combinations of world-state conditions, player actions and other acts' completion), and **placeholders** bound at instantiation (e.g. "X – the character who got into the accident"). An **Act Director** instantiates an act into a concrete sequence of (subject, action, motivation) tuples and then reviews it three ways: an LLM coherency check, **execution simulation in the game environment**, and a character-motivation check. It iterates at most 2 times or until the plan is executable. An LLM character simulator runs the world when no act is active. Model: gpt-4-0125-preview. The authors admit there is no systematic quantitative evaluation, and note latency and long-term-dependency problems. — [arXiv 2405.13042](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042v2)
- Drama Llama (Sun et al.), Jan 2025 **[PP]**. An LLM storylets framework. Each trigger has a **natural-language condition** (e.g. "Has Sepideh noticed Byron withdrawing…?"), a list of actions (stage directions injected into the story), and a type (Basic or Ending). After every message a minimal LLM drama manager checks the active triggers **sequentially with YES/NO-only prompts** and fires the first match. Reported "real-time generation within 3 seconds per step". In a study with 6 authors, 5/6 and 6/6 found the structure flexible, but only 3/6 found it original ("clichéd"), character development scored low (2–3/6), and triggers sometimes "did not work as expected". — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- Dramamancer (Wang, Sun, Wang, Roemmele, Chung, Kreminski; Midjourney), Wordplay@EMNLP 2025 **[PR workshop, DEMO]**. Schema = style + characters + scenes + **events as storylets (condition → outcome)**. Conditions are evaluated on playthrough state or fire after a line-count threshold. An *interpretation module* decides which events fired after each player input, and an *instantiation module* weaves the outcomes into the next line. The paper explicitly prioritizes responsiveness to recent player input over event satisfaction. No user-study numbers. — [arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1)
- NarrativeGenie (Kumaran, Rowe, Lester; NCSU), AIIDE 2024 **[PR]**. Turns a designer's story overview into a **partially ordered event graph** of narrative beats, then "dynamically direct[s] the narrative flow" at runtime from game state and player actions. The abstract reports coherence and alignment with designer intent; I found no numbers. — [AIIDE OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/31868)
- SceneCraft (Kumaran, Rowe, Mott, Lester), AIIDE 2023 **[PR]**. Natural-language scene objectives, NPC traits, location and variations go in. LLM chains produce branching NPC dialogue, extract emotions and gestures, and **convert the result into the game's scripting language** (a Python script object), re-querying when the output is not valid. Human eval with 9 participants on 7-point UEQ-derived items: ease of use 6.44, creative 5.76, adaptable 6.22, dependable 6.06, satisfying 6.19. — [AIIDE paper PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10475059)
- IBSEN (Han et al.), ACL 2024 **[PR]**. A **director agent** writes the plot outline, instructs actor agents, and **reschedules the plot when human players intervene** so that it still progresses toward the objectives. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.88)
- PANGeA (Buongiorno et al.; SMU Guildhall), AIIDE 2024 **[PR]**. Generates level data (setting, key items, NPCs with Big-5 personalities) from designer criteria. It includes a **memory system**, a **validation system that checks free-form player input against game rules** derived from the designer's criteria, a Unity plug-in, and a REST server supporting local and private LLMs. — [arXiv 2404.19721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.19721), [AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/31876)
- GENEVA (Microsoft Research), IEEE CoG 2024 **[PR]**. GPT-4 produces a branching beat DAG under explicit constraints (number of starts, endings and storylines; at most 3 consecutive shared beats; 2–3 beats shared by all storylines; unique beats ≥ 2× storylines). A second GPT-4 pass emits D3 graph code. Weaknesses: storylines lacked variation, and grounding was poor for settings that are rare in training data ("Quantum Realm"). — [arXiv 2311.09213](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09213v3)
- WHAT-IF (UPenn/UMBC), arXiv 2024–25 **[PP]**. GPT-4 meta-prompting branches a prewritten linear plot at the hero's key decisions. Each plot-tree node holds (state, goal, key decision, alternate decision), and the tree is stored outside the LLM as a graph. Evaluation is qualitative only (Iron Man case study). About 1 minute per branch, and the authors call public deployment infeasible on time and cost. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3)
- CALYPSO (Zhu, Martin, Head, Callison-Burch), AIIDE 2023 **[PR]**. An LLM supports a human DM instead of replacing them. DMs found it produced high-fidelity text ready for players and low-fidelity ideas they could develop. — [AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27534)
- 1001 Nights (Sun et al.), AIIDE 2023 **[PR]**. Keywords spoken in the co-created story (e.g. "sword") materialize as game items. GPT-4 reasoning is used for story consistency. — [arXiv 2308.12915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.12915)
- ChatRPG v1 vs v2 ("Static vs. Agentic Game Master AI…", Jørgensen et al.), Feb 2025 **[PP]**. Both use GPT-4. v1 is a single prompt with the full game state appended. v2 has a ReAct **Narrator** agent (tools for wounds, healing, combat) and an **Archivist** agent (tools that update character and environment state). Results in §5. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- Unbounded (Google), ICLR 2025 **[PR]**. A "generative infinite game" (life sim) driven by a distilled small LLM (details in §7). — [arXiv 2410.18975](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18975v2)

**Emergent events and agent societies**
- Generative Agents (Park et al.), UIST 2023 **[PR]**. 25 agents with a memory stream, reflection and planning. Seeded with the single idea of a Valentine's party, awareness grew from 1 agent (4%) to 13 (52%), 5 of 12 invitees showed up, and mayoral-candidacy awareness grew from 1 to 8 (32%). There were no total fabrications of who-knows-what, but there were "embellishments". The 2-day run of 25 agents cost "thousands of dollars in token credits" and took multiple days. — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2)
- Lyfe Agents (2023) **[PP]**. Option-action framework (high-level decisions are made rarely), asynchronous self-monitoring, and Summarize-and-Forget memory. Reported **10–100× lower cost** than existing alternatives. — [arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02172v1)
- Affordable Generative Agents (2024) **[PP]**. A "Lifestyle Policy" reuses responses to repeated situations, and "Social Memory" avoids regenerating repeated information. Claims the same performance at significantly lower cost. — [arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)
- Player-Driven Emergence in LLM-Driven Game Narrative (Peng et al., Microsoft), IEEE CoG 2024 **[PR]**. A mystery game with GPT-4 NPCs and 28 players. GPT-4 converted the logs into narrative node graphs, which revealed **emergent nodes** outside the authored narrative. Players who like discovery and exploration created the most. — [arXiv 2404.17027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17027)

**Story sifting**
- Lessard et al., "Narrative Gold Digging: Sifting and Narrating Stories from a Procedural Simulation", FDG 2026 **[PR]**. Formal sifting heuristics ("unexpectedness", "dramatic situations") picked 6 stories from a social-simulation log. Each was narrated once by a human and once by GPT-4, and 24 participants rated them. **The results on whether the heuristics made a difference were inconclusive.** GPT-4's narration smoothed over incoherent event chains (it treated an irrelevant funeral introspection seriously). The authors separate a "story effect" from a "narrative effect". — [PCG workshop archive PDF](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Kreminski's Winnow (AIIDE 2021) **[PR, pre-LLM]**. A DSL for **incremental** sifting patterns that detect "storyful" event sequences while they are still playing out. This is the symbolic baseline for "detect an emergent story in the event log, then promote it to a quest". — [AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18903)

**Overviews**
- "GPT for Games: An Updated Scoping Review (2020–2024)" **[PP]** is a broader map of the area (not read in detail). — [arXiv 2411.00308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00308)

### Inferences
- In practice, the director patterns that work all keep an **author-owned skeleton**: StoryVerse's abstract acts, Drama Llama and Dramamancer storylets, NarrativeGenie's partially ordered beats, IBSEN's outline, GENEVA's constrained DAG. The LLM does instantiation, timing and surface text. For Gothic this suggests "LLM instantiates authored quest *templates* or acts against live world state" rather than "LLM invents quests from nothing".
- No published system I found replaces a full, hand-scripted quest system in a shipped 3D RPG with fixed assets. Every system with evaluation is a text game, a prototype, a Unity demo or a tabletop assistant.

### Gaps
- I could not get full results for Ashby et al. (the ACM DL returned 403). I did not find which LM they used or their effect sizes.
- I found no peer-reviewed numbers for NarrativeGenie beyond its abstract claims.
- "LIGHT" quest work (Ammanabrolu et al. 2021, "How to Motivate Your Dragon") predates 2023, and I did not re-check it here.

## 2. Hybrid neuro-symbolic patterns: LLM proposes, symbolic system validates

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence comes from narrative-planning work. **A symbolic planner or validator plus an LLM as guide or generator beats the LLM alone on causal soundness and intentionality.** LLMs used as heuristics rarely beat classical heuristics, and their usefulness collapses when the domain vocabulary is unfamiliar. Runtime systems converge on one rule: **the LLM selects or parametrizes pre-programmed state transformations; it never writes state directly.**

### Cited Findings
- Farrell & Ware, "Large Language Models as Narrative Planning Search Guides", IEEE ToG 17(2), June 2025 **[PR]**. The Sabre narrative planner, with hand-written symbolic domains, describes the current state to gpt-3.5-turbo. The LLM suggests next events, and legal actions are ranked by embedding similarity to the suggestions (text-embedding-ada-002), which gives a "GPT-guided" uniform-cost search (UCS_GPT). In the Basketball problem (about 1,200-node space), UCS_GPT reached the solution in **31 nodes, 90% of them "explained"** (believable), while plain Sabre explored **over 4 million nodes**. UCS_GPT reached the goal sooner in all benchmarks. **Obfuscation test:** with all names replaced by unrelated words (same logic), it needed 72 nodes with 33% explained instead of 28 nodes with 75%, which is worse than the 37% baseline. The LLM's guidance depends on domain words implying a consistent genre and setting. The paper also warns that **an LLM introducing new story elements is a liability in 3D games with fixed assets** ("introduce a dragon … useless if the game does not have a rigged and animated dragon model"). — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/farrell2024large.pdf)
- Senanayake & Ware, "Language Models as Narrative Planning Heuristics", FDG 2025 **[PR]**. GPT-4o-mini was asked to complete partial stories and serve as a heuristic, compared with h+, hmax and relaxed-plan (h_rp) heuristics. "LLMs rarely exceed the accuracy of classical planning heuristics." First-correct-action accuracy: Hospital, best LLM variant 9.2% vs h_rp 49.6%; Deer Hunter, 69.3% vs 87.1%. The LLM won only in a few domains (Space, Treasure, partly Gramma). **API latency was "a prohibitive bottleneck to any kind of online search."** Code: github.com/lazzy07/llm_as_np_heuristic. — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/senanayake2025language.pdf)
- "Can LLMs Generate Good Stories? Insights and Challenges from a Narrative Planning Perspective", June 2025 **[PP]**. An **Answer Set Programming (Clingo) validator** checks LLM-generated plans for causal soundness, character intentionality and dramatic conflict. Success out of 30: Secret Agent (causal) GPT-4o 13, o1 30, Claude-3.5 26, Claude-3.7 30. Aladdin (intentionality) GPT-4o 0, o1 14, Claude-3.5 0, Claude-3.7 15. Western (conflict) GPT-4o 3, o1 20, Claude-3.5 2, Claude-3.7 6. **Reasoning models vastly outperform non-reasoning ones.** External ASP feedback improves results but adds compute cost, and performance drops sharply as the number of characters and the size of the delegation network grow. — [arXiv 2506.10161](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10161v1)
- Góngora, Chiruzzo, Méndez, Gervás, "World-State Transformations for Neuro-symbolic Interactive Storytelling", ICCC 2026 **[PR]**. The LLM interprets player input and **predicts which pre-programmed world-state transformation should fire**, while the symbolic side keeps the state consistent. Tested with Llama 3 70B and Gemini 1.5 Flash, in English and Spanish, with 8 participants and 2 scenarios. Conclusion: transformations maintain world-state consistency while still encouraging creative input. — [arXiv 2605.24719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24719)
- PAYADOR (same group), ICCC 2024 **[PR]**. Reframes the "world-update problem": the LLM is grounded on a **minimal structured world representation** and predicts the *outcome* (state changes) of a free-text action, instead of mapping input to predefined actions. Open source. — [arXiv 2504.07304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07304)
- "Real-Time World Crafting" (Drake & Dong), Wordplay@EMNLP 2025 **[PR workshop]**. The LLM translates natural language into a **constrained DSL** that configures an ECS at runtime, so it never emits raw code. Tested with Gemini, GPT and Claude. Chain-of-thought improved creative alignment, and **few-shot examples were needed for complex DSL scripts**. — [arXiv 2510.16952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16952)
- Structured Knowledge Trees for an LLM detective game (Rahmati & Zhao), Sept 2026 **[PP]**. Three agents (retrieval, dialogue, **verification**) on Llama-3.1-70B via Bedrock. A symbolic knowledge tree gates what the suspect may disclose in each narrative phase. Results in §5 and §6. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- Story2Game's precondition and effect annotations are converted into executable checks over a world graph (see §1). — [arXiv 2505.03547](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03547v1)
- Ashby et al. (CHI 2023): a **hand-crafted knowledge graph supplies quest content**, and the LM only verbalizes it. — [abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3544548.3581441?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,citationCount)
- PANGeA's validation system has the LLM judge free-form input against designer-derived game rules (LLM-as-validator, not symbolic). — [arXiv 2404.19721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.19721)
- RPGBench uses an automated **BFS validity checker** on LLM-created games to check reachability, valid rule progression and achievable endings. This is a cheap symbolic "can this quest actually be finished?" test (results in §5). — [arXiv 2502.00595](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1)
- From search snippets only (not read in full): the SINE pipeline (Lübeck) combines grammar-guided decoding, deterministic validation and a repair agent for interactive-fiction serious games — [Lübeck research portal](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/automated-generation-and-evaluation-of-interactive-fiction-seriou/). GAMED.AI (ACL 2026 demo) reports a 90% validation pass rate and 98.3% schema compliance using Pydantic schemas and deterministic "quality gates" — [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-demo.84/).

### Inferences
- In order of evidence strength, the patterns are:
  1. **LLM chooses from a closed menu of symbolic transformations or quest templates.** This was demonstrated at ICCC 2026 and mirrors the existing 2B "decider" menu pattern.
  2. **LLM generates structured JSON or DSL, a deterministic validator checks it, and failures are repaired or retried** (Story2Game, Real-Time World Crafting, SceneCraft, World-Gen→Quest-Line).
  3. **Planner with the LLM as soft guide**. This is useful offline, but latency rules out online search per Senanayake & Ware.
  4. **LLM as validator of the LLM** (PANGeA, StoryVerse coherency review). This is the weakest, because it shares the same blind spots.
- The obfuscation result matters for Gothic. Daedalus identifiers (`VLK_4201_Wirt`, `ItMi_Gold`) are meaningless tokens to an LLM. Every symbol exposed to the director should carry a natural-language gloss in the target language (Russian) plus lore context, otherwise guidance quality drops toward random.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed work that uses HTN planners with LLMs specifically for game quests in 2023–26. The PDDL/ASP work above is narrative-planning research on benchmark domains, not shipped games.
- I found no study of knowledge-graph constraint checking against an existing large RPG's lore (Gothic-scale), only small authored domains.

## 3. How quests are represented so the game can detect completion deterministically

### Takeaway
Every system that can verify progress represents a quest as **typed objectives or events with machine-checkable conditions over state variables** (location, inventory, attribute flags, prior-event completion, numeric bounds), plus explicit termination or ending conditions. Natural-language conditions judged by an LLM (Drama Llama, Dramamancer) are flexible but explicitly non-deterministic.

### Cited Findings
- Story2Game. Preconditions come in 3 groups: fundamental (location and inventory), additional (custom boolean or 0–10 integer attributes on objects), and preceding events. Effects come in 4 types: move, attribute modify, create, remove. The world is a graph of Player, Character, Item, Room and Container nodes with inventories. A known failure: rooms cannot hold attributes, which broke some generated actions. — [arXiv 2505.03547](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03547v1)
- RPGBench. A game is defined by **state variables** (numeric, with initial, min and max), **events** (entering condition, success condition, effects on variables) and **termination conditions** (boolean expressions for success or failure). Objective metrics are computed automatically: Event Condition Error and Variable Update Error. — [arXiv 2502.00595](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1)
- StoryVerse abstract acts: goal + prerequisites (world-state conditions, player actions, act-completion dependencies) + placeholders bound at runtime. — [arXiv 2405.13042](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042v2)
- Drama Llama triggers: a natural-language condition, judged YES/NO by an LLM after each message, plus actions and a type (Basic or Ending). Authors reported inconsistent trigger timing. — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- Dramamancer events: condition → outcome storylets, where a condition is either an LLM judgement on playthrough state or a line-count threshold (a deterministic fallback). — [arXiv 2601.18785](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18785v1)
- WHAT-IF nodes hold (state S, goal G, key decision KD, alternate decision AD), and edges hold decision → consequence → new state. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3)
- GENEVA and NarrativeGenie use beat DAGs and partially ordered event graphs: ordering constraints between beats, not state conditions. — [GENEVA](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09213v3), [NarrativeGenie](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/31868)
- ChatRPG v2: state (characters with HP, environments, history) is changed only through Archivist tool calls, and the Narrator calls tools for wounds, healing and combat. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- Ian Bicking's review of Hidden Door, an outside critique **[BLOG, 2025]**. Objectives appear as "cards" that are "unstable and ungrounded". He proposes making the LLM classify each choice against current objectives as SUCCESS, FAILURE or IMPOSSIBLE, with explicit consequences. — [ianbicking.org](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game)

### Inferences
- For Gothic: an LLM-issued quest should compile into the same primitives that Daedalus quests already use. That means a `MIS_*` state variable (LOG_RUNNING, SUCCESS, FAILED), a log topic and entries, and objectives expressed as checkable predicates the engine or server can evaluate without an LLM: has-item(npc, item, n), npc-dead, npc-knows-info, distance(npc, waypoint) < r, attitude, guild, chapter, time of day. (This mapping is my own inference from Gothic scripting conventions, not from a cited source.) The LLM should write only the flavour text, choose among template types, and bind parameters (which NPC, item or waypoint), and every binding should be validated against the live instance tables.
- Use LLM-judged natural-language conditions only for "soft" beats, such as "player persuaded X" in dialogue, where the existing dialogue LLM is already the judge. Give them deterministic fallbacks: a turn or time threshold like Dramamancer's line count, or a hard predicate.

### Gaps
- I found no published schema for LLM-generated quests that includes failure branches, timeouts, quest-giver death handling or reward balancing. These seem to be left to the engine designer.

## 4. Long-term plot memory and consistency: state summaries, retrieval, lore grounding, story bibles

### Takeaway
Consistency failures come mainly from (a) state that lives only in the context window and (b) retrieval misses. The working mitigations are: an **explicit external state owned by code or a dedicated archivist**, **hierarchical summaries plus keyword- or embedding-triggered lore cards**, and **phase-gated knowledge** (the LLM can only reveal what the current state allows).

### Cited Findings
- ChatRPG v1, with the full game state appended to every prompt, showed "story coherence degradation as context window filled" and **forgot recently picked-up items**. v2, with a separate Archivist agent updating state through tools, scored significantly higher on story coherence (p=0.040) among other dimensions. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- Generative Agents: the most common errors were **failure to retrieve relevant memories**, **embellishment** (e.g. inventing that someone "is going to make an announcement tomorrow") and overly formal speech. Agents rarely fabricated whole memories outright. — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2)
- AI Dungeon (Latitude docs) **[BLOG/docs]**. The context combines AI Instructions, **Plot Essentials** (always-on facts), Author's Note and **Story Cards**. Story cards are lore entries included only when their keyword triggers appear, prioritized by recency and frequency, and packed until space runs out. A **Memory Bank** of auto-summaries of story chunks is retrieved by relevance (embeddings). The docs say context length is limited by cost. — [What goes into the context](https://latitudegames.notion.site/What-goes-into-the-Context-sent-to-the-AI-7e5881352de6427da9c92b04149e8e50), [What is Memory](https://latitudegames.notion.site/What-is-Memory-f2bc3e77bb1b48aca3716aeba21b8d21)
- SCORE (2025) **[PP]**. Combines dynamic state tracking (objects and characters via symbolic logic), hierarchical episode summaries and hybrid TF-IDF + embedding retrieval. Reports +23.6% coherence (NCI-2.0), 89.7% emotional consistency and **41.8% fewer hallucinations** than baseline GPT. These are self-defined metrics, so treat them with caution. — [arXiv 2503.23512](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23512v6)
- Structured Knowledge Trees: the hallucination ratio (violations of the authored narrative) fell from **17.80% (LLM-only) to 6.27%**, a 64.78% relative decrease, and premature disclosure of story secrets was entirely prevented. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- WHAT-IF stores the plot tree externally to "keep track of the story for prompting" and to prevent drift. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3)
- "Can Language Models Serve as Text-Based World Simulators?" (ACL 2024) **[PR]**. GPT-4's accuracy on ByteSized32 state transitions **does not exceed 59.9% for transitions with a non-trivial state change**. It struggles with arithmetic, commonsense and scientific transitions. This is direct evidence against letting the LLM be the world-state tracker. — [arXiv 2406.06485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06485)
- GENEVA grounding works for familiar settings (Minecraft, the 21st century) and **fails for settings sparse in training data**. — [arXiv 2311.09213](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09213v3)
- Hidden Door's public positioning **[company claims, not independently verified]**: storytelling is split into many small AI tasks, combining structured narrative beats with a **database of tropes and world rules**, modular plot structures, pre-generated and hand-written elements, and a "story governor". — [AI Game Changers interview](https://aigamechangers.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-not-itself-creative), [Hidden Door jobs page](https://www.hiddendoor.co/jobs/senior-software-ml-engineer-story-systems). **This is contradicted in practice** by Bicking's play review: in his reading, the story is "written only as far as it's been presented to the player", the world state is mostly implicit and inconsistent (scenes flip between inside and outside a car), and the prompt is visibly built from cards. — [ianbicking.org](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game)

### Inferences
- Gothic lore is niche and mostly Russian or German fan-wiki material. GENEVA's sparse-setting failure and Farrell & Ware's obfuscation result both imply the director needs an explicit **story bible**: factions, chapters, key NPC fates, locations, and who knows what. Inject it through keyword-triggered cards, in the AI Dungeon pattern, rather than trusting pretraining.
- The existing per-save NPC memory is the right substrate, but quest and plot state should be a separate **authoritative, code-owned ledger**: open quests, bound entities, facts introduced, promises made. The director reads it, and it is updated only through validated tool calls, which is the ChatRPG v2 Archivist pattern.

### Gaps
- I found no study that measures plot consistency over 10+ hour play sessions. The longest evaluated sessions were about 20–40 minutes (SKT, ChatRPG).
- I found no evaluation of lore grounding for non-English game lore.

## 5. Evaluation: metrics, user studies and measured results

### Takeaway
Evaluations are small (N = 6 to 33, except Värtinen's 349), often LLM-judged, and mostly short-session. Robust findings: structured or agentic architectures beat single-prompt ones on coherence and player experience; automatic mechanics metrics show even top models break rules in 25–90% of rounds; and **LLM judges overrate LLM stories**.

### Cited Findings
- Värtinen et al. (IEEE ToG 2024): N=349 players; about 20% of GPT-2 quests were acceptable, with high variance. — [Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/generating-role-playing-game-quests-with-gpt-language-models/)
- ChatRPG (N=12, Player Experience Inventory plus custom items). The agentic v2 was significantly better on 9 of 14 constructs: ease of control p=.012, goals and rules p=.018, curiosity p=.047, mastery p=.004, immersion p=.034, **story coherence p=.040**, story interestingness p=.0187, replay p=.050, satisfaction p=.0468. Mean scores: v1 0.68–2.17, v2 1.97–2.81. The authors credit **few-shot examples in tool descriptions more than ReAct itself**. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- RPGBench. **Game Creation** validity check rate (VCR): GPT-4o 0.49 (best), DeepSeek-V3 0.38, Gemini 1.5 Pro 0.04, so even the best model produced a valid, finishable game only about half the time. **Game Simulation** mechanic score (share of rounds with no mechanical error) ranged 0.113–0.765. The best was Gemini 2.0 Flash Exp at 0.765; Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 0.113 (variable-update error 0.308). The authors conclude that LLMs "can produce engaging stories but often struggle to implement consistent, verifiable game mechanics". — [arXiv 2502.00595](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1)
- SceneCraft: N=9 designers, 7-point UEQ-derived items, 5.76–6.44. ROUGE-L between generations was used as a diversity proxy, and embedding similarity to the prompt as an intent-alignment proxy (p=2.6e-62 vs unrelated scripts). — [AIIDE PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10475059)
- Drama Llama: N=6 authors. Strong on structure and pacing, weak on originality (3/6) and character development (2–3/6). — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- GamePlot (Microsoft, 14 game designers) **[PP]**: high satisfaction and sense of ownership, but the study "reconfirms" that LLMs are limited at complex and truly innovative content. — [arXiv 2411.02714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02714)
- Player-Driven Emergence: N=28. A novel metric counts emergent narrative nodes found by GPT-4 graph extraction from logs. — [arXiv 2404.17027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17027)
- SKT detective study: N=33, within-subjects, two 20-minute sessions. LLM-only: 10/33 players forced a full confession (the mystery collapsed), at an average of 33.6 turns. SKT: 0/33. 24/33 progressed through the contradiction mechanics, at an average of 6.79 prompts per contradiction. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- Generative Agents: believability was ranked by 100 evaluators and converted to TrueSkill, and an ablation showed that observation, planning and reflection each matter. — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2)
- "Spoiler Alert" (Sui et al., COLM 2026) **[PR]**. A "100-Endings" tension metric: at each sentence, a model predicts the ending 100 times, and tension is the mismatch with the true ending. LLM stories are **significantly more predictable** than human ones. **On EQ-Bench, LLM judges rank zero-shot AI stories above New Yorker stories**, while the 100-Endings metric correctly ranks New Yorker stories far higher. Structural constraints in generation raised the measured tension. — [arXiv 2604.09854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09854)
- Lessard et al. (FDG 2026): 24 raters compared human and GPT-4 narrations of sifted stories. The heuristics' effect was inconclusive. — [PDF](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- Unbounded: GPT-4 as judge across character-state tracking, environment relevance, narrative coherence and instruction following (numbers in §7). — [arXiv 2410.18975](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18975v2)

### Inferences
- A Gothic director should be evaluated with **objective mechanics metrics first**. RPGBench-style metrics translate to: share of issued quests that are completable (BFS/validator), share of state updates that are valid, and share of quests that reference only existing instances. Run these offline over many simulated saves. Use LLM-as-judge only for prose, given the Spoiler Alert finding.

### Gaps
- No study measured **repetitiveness over many generated quests** in a single long campaign, beyond SceneCraft's ROUGE-L pairs and GENEVA's qualitative "lacked variation".
- No study measured **fun or retention** for LLM-generated side quests against authored ones in a shipped 3D RPG.

## 6. Known failure modes and mitigations

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are sycophancy and pushover adjudication, impossible or ungrounded objectives, invented entities with no assets, state drift as context grows, cliché and predictability, and latency or cost. The mitigations that have numbers behind them are symbolic gating of state and disclosure (SKT), agent separation with tool-only state writes (ChatRPG v2), validators with retry (Story2Game, SceneCraft), and reasoning models plus external validators for plan soundness.

### Cited Findings
- **Sycophancy and pushover GM.** LLM-only detective NPCs "sycophantically" accept the player's premise; 10/33 players extracted a full confession, typically ending the mystery in under 20 minutes. Symbolic phase gating brought this to 0/33. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- **Persuasive-rhetoric attacks on rule adjudication.** CoC-Seduce (Call of Cthulhu) has 22 adjudicator models, 5,376 samples, 4 settings and 16 skill categories. **Pseudo-logic framing is the most effective manipulation**, and "neither newer releases nor explicit reasoning reliably confer adjudication robustness". Note that the arXiv title changed between versions ("Seduced by the Narrative…" in search results; "Assessing Rule Adherence of LLM Adjudicators in Call of Cthulhu TRPG" on the abstract page). **[PP]** — [arXiv 2607.02802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02802)
- **Over-compliance and no meaningful failure.** ChatRPG v1 let the narrative deviate significantly from player intent and produced bad combat outcomes. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1). Bicking on Hidden Door: "I haven't failed at any action yet"; "the LLM always says you guessed right"; objectives materialize and resolve instantly; trivial details are over-indexed (mismatched socks become "practically a character"); there is unearned emotional weight; impossible actions are accepted. — [ianbicking.org](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game)
- **Impossible and invalid content.** In RPGBench Game Creation, about half or more of LLM-generated games fail the validity check (best VCR 0.49). In Story2Game, 12.5–37.5% of stories fail to fully compile, and about 40% of dynamically generated actions are semantically wrong. The main cause is object misidentification. — [RPGBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1), [Story2Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03547v1)
- **Assets that don't exist.** An LLM introducing new story elements (a dragon) is useless in 3D games with fixed assets. — [Farrell & Ware](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/farrell2024large.pdf)
- **World-state tracking errors.** GPT-4 is at most 59.9% accurate on non-trivial state transitions. — [arXiv 2406.06485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06485). Top RPGBench simulators still make mechanical errors in about 24% or more of rounds. — [RPGBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1)
- **Long-horizon incoherence.** StoryVerse authors report LLMs struggle with "preserving long-term dependency and coherence". — [arXiv 2405.13042](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042v2). Single-prompt ChatRPG v1 degraded as the context filled. — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- **Plan soundness at scale.** Non-reasoning models fail intentionality almost completely (GPT-4o 0/30 on Aladdin), and all models degrade as characters are added. — [arXiv 2506.10161](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10161v1)
- **Cliché and predictability.** Drama Llama outputs were judged "clichéd" (originality 3/6). — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1). LLM stories are measurably more predictable (lower tension). — [arXiv 2604.09854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09854). GENEVA storylines lacked variation. — [arXiv 2311.09213](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09213v3). WHAT-IF lists non-choices, illogical decisions violating character constraints, "retreat" decisions and stagnation. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3)
- **Trigger and pacing unreliability.** Drama Llama authors reported triggers "did not work as expected". — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1). Narrative pacing collapsed in the LLM-only detective game. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- **Provider content filters.** In ChatRPG v2, OpenAI content filters blocked fantasy combat descriptions mid-game. Tool-description wording also changed behaviour significantly (prompt sensitivity). — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19519v1)
- **Cost and latency.** Generative Agents: "thousands of dollars" for 25 agents over 2 game-days. — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442v2). WHAT-IF: about 1 minute per branch, too expensive to release. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3). LLM-in-the-loop planning: API latency is "a prohibitive bottleneck" for online search. — [Senanayake & Ware](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/senanayake2025language.pdf). StoryVerse notes latency from frequent LLM calls. — [arXiv 2405.13042](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042v2)
- **LLM narration masks bad story material.** GPT-4 narrated an incoherent sifted chain as if it were meaningful. — [Lessard et al. FDG 2026](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/lessard2026narrative.pdf)
- **Mitigation proposals from practice.** Bicking proposes writing outlines before prose, structured prompts that make the LLM state world context explicitly, consequence summaries for each choice, SUCCESS/FAILURE/IMPOSSIBLE classification against objectives, and **starting from fully pre-generated static choice fiction** before going real-time. — [ianbicking.org](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game)

### Inferences
- Sycophancy is the most dangerous failure for a Gothic story director. The player, through the dialogue LLM, can talk NPCs into giving rewards, completing quests or revealing secrets. Quest completion and reward grants must be **predicates checked by code**, never the dialogue model's opinion. The CoC-Seduce result says a better or reasoning model will not fix this by itself.
- Asset-bound generation (only existing NPC, item, waypoint and animation instances) eliminates the "dragon" class of failures structurally, and it is cheap to validate against Daedalus symbol tables.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study of **pacing** in LLM-directed quest sequences (e.g. quest frequency or overload) beyond qualitative reports.
- I found no published data on how often LLM directors contradict canonical plot events of an existing franchise.

## 7. Small or local models vs. large cloud models; offline pre-generation vs. real-time

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a split. **Large or reasoning models** handle the rare, high-stakes planning: arc and quest design, offline or slow-path (reasoning models win big on plan soundness). **Small fine-tuned or distilled models** handle frequent, narrowly scoped runtime calls: trigger checks, choosing among options, state-change prediction, short text. Distillation from a large teacher on synthetic data is the proven recipe, and pre-generation is repeatedly recommended to control both quality and latency.

### Cited Findings
- Unbounded (ICLR 2025) **[PR]**. **Gemma-2B distilled from GPT-4o** on 5,000 synthetic multi-round interactions (a "world simulation LLM" and a "user LLM" talking to each other, diversity-filtered at ROUGE-L < 0.7), trained for 6,500 steps on 4×A100. About **1-second refresh latency**, against about 5 s for a 7B model. A GPT-4 judge scored the distilled model **comparable to GPT-4o** (fetched summary: 7.82 vs 7.68 overall). It tracks hunger, energy, fun and hygiene state variables. Caveats: LLM-judged, and a narrow domain. — [arXiv 2410.18975](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18975v2)
- Munk, Valdivia, Burelli, "High-quality generation of dynamic game content via small language models: A proof of concept", 2026 (DOI prefix matches the FDG '26 proceedings) **[PR, probable]**. Previous small-model studies gave "poor output quality". Their recipe: **aggressive fine-tuning on deliberately scoped tasks** with DAG-based synthetic data, plus a **retry-until-success loop with LLM evaluation**, gives adequate quality and "predictable" real-time latency in a minimal RPG loop ("rhetorical battles of reputations"). — [arXiv 2601.23206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23206)
- Lyfe Agents: 10–100× cost reduction from making high-level (expensive) decisions rarely and executing low-level actions cheaply, plus summarize-and-forget memory. — [arXiv 2310.02172](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02172v1)
- Affordable Generative Agents: cache and reuse repeated behaviour ("Lifestyle Policy") to cut cost at equal performance. — [arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)
- Reasoning models (o1, Claude-3.7) vastly outperform GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 on narrative-plan soundness and intentionality (e.g. 14–15/30 vs 0/30). — [arXiv 2506.10161](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10161v1)
- RPGBench: model rankings flip between game creation (GPT-4o best) and simulation (Gemini 2.0 Flash best), so a single model is not best at every pipeline stage. — [arXiv 2502.00595](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00595v1)
- Real-Time World Crafting: larger models captured creative intent better, and few-shot examples were needed for complex DSL output. — [arXiv 2510.16952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16952)
- Drama Llama reached about 3 s per step with a cloud LLM doing sequential YES/NO trigger checks, which is the per-turn budget a cheap local classifier could take over. — [arXiv 2501.09099](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09099v1)
- An SKT-style tri-agent pipeline ran on Llama-3.1-70B; a mid-size open model was enough once symbolic gating was in place. — [arXiv 2609.23043](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23043)
- World-State Transformations: comparable use of Llama 3 70B and Gemini 1.5 Flash as the "which transformation fires" predictor. — [arXiv 2605.24719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24719)
- Pre-generation: Bicking recommends starting from "entirely pregenerated" choice fiction. — [ianbicking.org](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game). Hidden Door describes "pre-generating and handwriting narrative bits that are tropes" (company claim). — [AI Game Changers interview](https://aigamechangers.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-not-itself-creative). WHAT-IF's about 1 min per branch implies branches must be generated ahead of time, not on demand. — [arXiv 2412.10582](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10582v3)
- Small fine-tuned models for lore-coherent RPG content exist in 2025 venues, e.g. "Fine-tuning an LLM to Generate Lore Coherent Encounters for Dungeons and Dragons" (PACLIC 2025), but I could not retrieve details (404). — [ACL Anthology search hit](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2025.paclic-1.65)

### Inferences (mapped to the current Gothic stack)
- **Slow path (cloud "gpt-6.1-sol", about 3 s TTFT, subscription):** chapter- or arc-level director ticks, run on events (quest completed, chapter change, location entered, NPC died) or every N in-game hours, not per frame. It fills or creates quest instances from templates, writes their text, and proposes event injections, all as schema-constrained tool calls. Prefer pre-generating 1–3 candidate quests per hub ahead of need (while the player is elsewhere) so that a 3–10 s generation never blocks gameplay. Reasoning-model results suggest giving this tier extended reasoning when it designs multi-step quests.
- **Fast path (local 2B decider, about 100 ms):** per-tick or per-utterance classification. That covers "did this soft trigger fire?" (replacing Drama Llama's YES/NO cloud checks), "which pre-authored transformation or event fits now?" (the World-State Transformations pattern), and NPC reaction choice. Unbounded and Munk et al. support distilling such narrow tasks from the cloud model's logs into the 2B model.
- **Deterministic core (server code plus Daedalus or engine hooks):** quest state machine, objective predicates, reward grants, entity binding validation, story-bible ledger, sifting patterns over the event log (a Winnow-like DSL) that nominate emergent situations for the director. Neither model should be able to mark a quest complete or grant an item except through validated tool calls.
- **Replacing hand-written Daedalus main quests is not supported by the evidence.** Every evaluated system relies on an authored skeleton, and plan soundness, mechanics adherence and long-horizon coherence remain weak even for frontier models. The evidence fits better with **augmenting** the authored main plot with LLM-instantiated side quests and events within strict schemas.

### Gaps
- I found no head-to-head study of a 1–3B local model vs a frontier cloud model on the *director* role (quest design), as opposed to simulation or dialogue.
- I found no published latency or cost budgets for an LLM director running alongside a real-time 3D RPG. The closest data points are Drama Llama (about 3 s per step, text game) and Unbounded (about 1 s, 2B, 2D life sim).
